## Fader Knob Fit Analysis

### Background

We received a batch of fader knobs that varied noticeably in how well they
fit the fader tangs. To investigate, the knobs were sorted by hand-feel into
three groups and then measured with hand-held calipers (see **Fader and Knob Measurements** below for raw numbers).

### Groups

#### Pink — Poor fit
- Too loose
- Falls off easily
- Bottoms out on the tang
- Rubs paint off the enclosure

#### Orange — Marginal fit
- Stays on acceptably
- Bottoms out on the tang
- Rubs paint off the enclosure

#### Blue — Good fit
- Stays on well
- Rides above the enclosure (no contact)
- No rubbing, no paint wear

### Measurements

Eight fader tangs were measured to look for variation. There was some 0.5mm variation in height, but length and width were very consistent.  


Four parameters were captured on each fader knob cavity (width, length, depth notch, depth shelf) using hand-held calipers.  (See dimensions page below for naming clarification)

There were no glaring dimensional differences between the groups, except that
the best-fitting knobs tended to have a slightly more narrow opening in the
**width** dimension, knobs with width below 2.29mm fit tightly. 


### Experiments

#### 1. Cross-section to check internal taper

Theorizing that the width might taper slightly tighter inside the knobs
whose measured opening width was under 2.28 mm, I cut one of the pink
(loose) units in half with diagonal cutters to inspect the internal profile.

When I refit the half onto a fader tang to verify the original loose
behavior, it now fit tight. The act of cutting deformed the plastic enough to change the fit substantially.  

#### 2. Light squeeze with pliers

I then took two loosely-fitting (pink) knobs and gave each a light squeeze
with pliers in the top half of the knob. After squeezing, these knobs were indistinguishable
from the blue good-fitting knobs and even noticeably tighter.  

### Questions for the Knob Engineers

The plastic seems to be easily deformed from a small mechanical
squeeze, and that deformation is enough to convert a loose knob into a
tight-fitting one. Questions are:

**Is this squeezing process safe as a production technique?**

Specifically:
Is a squeezed knob likely to retain its tight fit over the life of the product,
   or will the plastic quickly creep / relax back toward its molded geometry?  

 **Or, should we bin for tight-fitting knobsbefore any mechanical force is added?**



Based on these tests, it does not look like any change to the tool is needed, but the array of knob fits as-received is not workable without some intervention before loading the faders.
